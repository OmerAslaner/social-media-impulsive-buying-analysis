## 📌 Project Overview
This repository contains the data analysis and academic findings for our research on how social media usage and consumption behavior are interconnected. Specifically, the project investigates the effects of **Social Media Exposure** and **Influencer Credibility** on **Impulsive Buying Behavior**.

## 🔬 Methodology
*   **Target Population:** Active social media users (Aged 18+)
*   **Sample Size:** 205 participants
*   **Sampling Method:** Convenience sampling
*   **Data Collection:** Online survey (Google Forms) using validated 5-point Likert scales.

## 📊 Key Statistical Findings (SPSS)
1.  **Reliability:** All measurement scales demonstrated high internal consistency (Cronbach's Alpha > 0.70).
2.  **Correlation (H1 & H2):** Significant positive correlations exist between Social Media Exposure & Impulsive Buying ($r = 0.397, p < .001$), and Influencer Credibility & Impulsive Buying ($r = 0.421, p < .001$).
3.  **Regression (H3 & H4):** Social media exposure explains 15.7% of the variance in impulsive buying, while influencer credibility explains 17.8%. Both are significant predictors.
4.  **Gender Differences (H7 & H8):** Female participants reported significantly higher levels of both social media exposure and impulsive buying behavior compared to males (Independent Samples t-Test).
5.  **Platform Preferences (H6):** Chi-Square tests revealed significant gender differences: Females use TikTok more, whereas males dominate Twitter and YouTube. Instagram usage showed no significant gender disparity.

## 💡 Business & Marketing Implications
Social media is a powerful catalyst for consumption. Higher exposure and perceived credibility of influencers directly increase impulsive buying tendencies. Brands targeting female demographics should prioritize credible influencer collaborations on visual-first platforms like TikTok, given their higher susceptibility to impulsive, hedonic shopping.
